## 2. What the baseline got right

The Formal RFP remained a poor target motion: project viability passed while target viability failed. Chapter 15 exposed substantial acquisition work, and Chapter 16 showed why elapsed congestion and scarce capacity matter independently of delivery margin. Thus the answer to **“was the original verdict correct under its baseline motion?” is YES**. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT]`

## 3. What the baseline could not see

It could not distinguish customer category from motion, authority, purchasing path, contract size, or ownership. Chapters 5–14 varied those mechanisms without changing customer category. Some normalized motions pass and some fail, so category-wide rejection does not follow.

## 4. Formal RFP result

Formal solicitation retained high pre-award technical and proposal work, a long elapsed cycle, and closed-access exposure. This is the strongest evidence for the hypothesis. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 4, 15, 16, 19]`

## 5. Pilot result

The cooperative paid pilot established viable bounded economics in the model, but depended materially on sponsor strength and proved neither rollout nor a general market. It weakens, but does not falsify by itself, the hypothesis. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 5, 15, 16, 19]`

## 6. Read-only result

Read-only access removed authoritative-write work and reduced governance surface, while retaining mapping, export, reporting, access-expiration, and common review work. It addresses less value but creates a responsible bounded option. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 6, 12, 13]`

## 7. Configuration/incumbent result

Configuration solves much of the burden with lower custom ownership. More importantly, Chapter 14's adequate supported incumbent module wins the existing feasibility → economics → adequacy → ownership comparison. Custom software did **not** beat that supported alternative in the baseline; therefore the capstone is `CONFIGURE / BUY`, even though custom motions can be sellable. A custom edge is justified only if discovery proves a bounded material residual with supported access. `[MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION and OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 7, 14]`

## 8. Small-versus-large contract result

The small engagement misses the modeled contribution floor because fixed acquisition effort consumes limited value. A larger justified scope can improve per-deal economics, but adds delivery commitment and does not erase access, scope-justification, or pipeline constraints. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 8–9]`

## 9. Partner result

Partner-led work reduces seller-owned acquisition effort but shifts customer relationship, frontline work, and dependency to the partner while adding channel cost. It is a credible conditional path—not proof that buyers generally prefer partners. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 10, 12, 15]`

## 10. Existing-purchasing-path result

Procurement-path friction was material: reusing a purchasing mechanism removes or reduces stages and improves direct economics. But it does not automatically create buyer access, supported technical access, security acceptance, scope agreement, or contract resolution. Procurement, buyer access, technical governance, and contracting are separate mechanisms. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapter 11]`

## 11. Governance result

Security, accessibility, and governance are not a synonym for bureaucracy. Schema controls, data handling, testing, accessibility implementation, incident preparation, and supported-interface design are **legitimate delivery work**. Questionnaires, review coordination, evidence submission, approval waiting, and contract allocation are **acquisition/approval work**. Read-only removes write-specific work; configuration shifts some work to the incumbent; partner-led shifts some to a partner. Common requirements remain rather than magically disappearing. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapter 12]`

## 12. Closed-access result

```text
NO SUPPORTED ACCESS
CAN OVERRIDE
GOOD COMMERCIAL ECONOMICS
```

Bypass is never an option. Responsible fallbacks are configure, approved read-only/export, manual-assisted delivery, incumbent capability, or `NO DEAL`. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapter 13]`

## 13. Acquisition-economics result

The largest baseline burdens live in pre-award technical interpretation/response, proposal and procurement/contract coordination—not implementation alone. Pilot scope reduces them; an existing path removes procurement stages; a partner shifts part of the burden and charges for the channel. Formal RFP and some broad custom motions remain expensive despite potentially adequate delivery economics. Acquisition cost remains separate from delivery cost. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapter 15]`

## 14. Throughput result

Per-deal contribution and annualized contribution answer different questions. Bounded pilot, partner, and existing-path profiles use scarce acquisition capacity differently; long, bursty work can defer opportunities. A large engagement can contribute more per deal and still congest the annual pipeline. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapter 16]`

## 15. Repeatability result

Technical reuse covers canonical models, reporting shells, mappings, and tests. Same-account commercial reuse can include sponsor introductions, documents, and a purchasing path. Cross-customer commercial reuse is weaker because buyer access, sponsors, contracting, governance approval, and purchasing paths reset. Chapter 17 supports **repeatable project** where reuse is multi-dimensional. Chapter 18 remains conditional across governments. The finding is not a product and does not establish a repeatable market motion. `[OBSERVED LAB RESULT: Chapters 17–18]`

## 16. Evidence for `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER`

- Formal RFP target viability fails despite a viable project.
- Closed access defeats broad custom economics.
- Cross-government access, sponsor, purchasing, governance, and contracting work resets.
- Small contracts can remain acquisition-uneconomic.

## 17. Evidence against `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER`

- A sponsored bounded paid pilot passes modeled target gates.
- Read-only/configuration reduce authority and ownership.
- Partner-led and existing-path experiments reduce or reassign specific acquisition burdens.
- Same-account evidence supports multi-dimensional repeatable project delivery.

All are fictional `OBSERVED LAB RESULT` statements, not market claims.

The deterministic engine stops at the first applicable rule: (1) foundational failure → `NO DEAL`; (2) adequate native/incumbent and immaterial residual → `CONFIGURE / BUY`; (3) bounded material supported residual → `NARROW CUSTOM EDGE`; (4) direct failure plus viable partner → `PARTNER-LED TARGET`; (5) direct failure plus viable paid pilot → `PILOT-FIRST TARGET`; (6) multi-dimensional multi-engagement reuse → `REPEATABLE PROJECT`; (7) passing project and credible motion → `PROMISING — VALIDATE IN DISCOVERY`; (8) passing project but all credible motions fail → `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER`; (9) otherwise → `INVESTIGATE`.

This ordering prevents repeatability from overriding an adequate incumbent and prevents a commercial motion from overriding technical failure. It has no score and no hidden motion ranking.

## 20. Final verdict

The final verdict is **`CONFIGURE / BUY`**, triggered by rule 2. The evidence posture is **strong within the lab; conditional outside it**. This solution verdict coexists with the motion conclusion: Formal RFP is a poor target motion, while the category-level hypothesis is conditional.

## 21. Recommended engagement posture

Do not target generic Formal RFPs. Prefer supported incumbent configuration. Only if discovery proves a material residual, prefer a sponsored paid/read-only bounded edge, existing purchasing path, or acceptable partner. Reject closed access, unsupported writes, and small contracts whose acquisition economics fail.

## 22. What would need real discovery

Validate sponsor authority; purchasing path and contracting effort; incumbent capability and residual value; supported access; security, accessibility, and governance requirements; burden, value, willingness to pay, and sales effort; partner availability and ownership terms; and technical plus commercial repeatability across customers.

## 23. Final lesson

```text
GOOD PROBLEM ≠ GOOD PROJECT ≠ GOOD TARGET
≠ GOOD ENGAGEMENT MOTION ≠ REPEATABLE MARKET
```

Start with the problem, prefer adequate existing capability, require supported access, bound residual scope, test the real acquisition path, and only then select a verdict. The repository ends here; it makes no real-government or product claim.
