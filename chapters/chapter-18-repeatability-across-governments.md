# Chapter 18 — Repeatability Across Governments

## 1. The third engagement is the market test

Chapter 17's second department retained an account relationship. This chapter removes that advantage. **Second department success ≠ repeatable market.** Every locality, system, person, rule, price, and result here is fictional; this is not empirical public-sector evidence.

## 2. Same account versus new customer

`WITHIN_CUSTOMER` and `CROSS_CUSTOMER` are explicit scopes. Known stakeholders, a purchasing path, governance history, shared systems, support relationships, and sponsor referrals can help within an account. The baseline grants none of them to a new customer.

## 3. Meet the second fictional government

Wholly fictional **Blue Ridge County Development Services Department** moves applications through intake, completeness validation, discipline review, correction, technical approval, issuance, closure, and portfolio reporting. Its fictional **MunicipalWorks Development Platform** offers a rate-limited read-only case API without attachments. This differs from James River County's fictional CivicFlow/export arrangement (`MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION`).

## 4. What changed?

Blue Ridge has centralized IT, a separate security reviewer, early finance authority, procurement-controlled vendor contact, limited initial sponsorship, and an incumbent API dependency. Its fictional competitive-services solicitation does not inherit James River's path. County hosting, 90-day source retention, service-account review, and separate accessibility acceptance mix easier delivery (no write-control implementation) with harder documentation and approval.

## 5. Technical reuse

The canonical record, provenance, normalization framework, test harness, report shell, and configuration method travel to varying degrees. The API adapter, actual field mapping, customer records, and acceptance expectations do not. The executable inventory calculates **144 greenfield engineering hours, 81 required hours, and 63 saved hours** (`OBSERVED LAB RESULT`). Adaptation never becomes zero-cost reuse.

## 6. Discovery reuse

Interview templates, access checklists, burden categories, configuration inventory, and economics method travel. Actual workflow findings, burden values, stakeholder authority, system capability, and access approval reset. The baseline retains 26 discovery hours.

## 7. Buyer-access reset

Prospecting, qualification, sponsor creation, and authority mapping are new. A reusable sales checklist is not buyer access.

## 8. Procurement reset

The SOW method adapts; contract authorization is rebuilt. **Reusing a procurement artifact is not reusing the actual purchasing path.** Baseline acquisition totals 104 hours across Chapter 15's prospecting, qualification, discovery, proposal, procurement support, contract coordination, and governance-approval-support categories.

## 9. Governance-document reuse

The security architecture document adapts and the accessibility harness travels. Their explicit hours remain visible.

## 10. Governance-approval reset

Security approval and customer accessibility acceptance rebuild. A document is evidence submitted to a decision, not the decision itself.

## 11. Support reuse

Monitoring, logging, deployment tooling, and the runbook shell save 17 of 22 modeled hours. Credentials, mappings, data source, contacts, SLA expectations, workflow exceptions, and incumbent escalation retain 13 of 18 hours. Total incremental support is 18 hours.

## 12. First department versus second department versus second government

The `repeatability-matrix` command shows raw engineering, acquisition, governance, support, elapsed days, and contribution for Level 1 (first department), Level 2 (same-government second department), and Level 3 (new government). It calculates no repeatability score. The new motion's 104 acquisition hours versus 42 for Department 2 adds 62 hours of pipeline burden under Chapter 16's existing capacity concept; no new throughput model is introduced.

## 13. Scenario: technical reuse, commercial reset

The hard reset preserves baseline engineering while adding buyer, sponsor, contracting, procurement, and approval work. Its result is `INVESTIGATE`: technical repeatability can coexist with poor commercial economics.

## 14. Scenario: commercially friendly second government

A strong sponsor and fictional direct small-project path reduce—but do not erase—new-customer work. With explicit contribution and work thresholds satisfied, this sensitivity reaches `REPEATABLE PROJECT`.

## 15. Scenario: technical variation

Good buyer access and purchasing reduce acquisition work while divergent source and status models increase delivery adaptation. A good sales motion cannot guarantee delivery reuse.

## 16. What actually travels across governments?

```text
FIRST GOVERNMENT
      |
      +--> CODE / TESTS MAY TRAVEL
      +--> DOCUMENTS MAY ADAPT
      +--> CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP AND PURCHASING PATH DO NOT TRANSFER
      v
NEW GOVERNMENT
      +--> BUYER ACCESS RESETS
      +--> PROCUREMENT RESETS
      +--> APPROVAL RESETS
```

## 17. Repeatable project versus repeatable market motion

```text
TECHNICAL REPEATABILITY
        ≠
COMMERCIAL REPEATABILITY
        ≠
MARKET REPEATABILITY
```

The baseline derives `INVESTIGATE`, not a repeatable-market claim. Engineering reuse, discovery method, acquisition, procurement, governance, support, customer value, contribution, and elapsed cycle all matter. `CROSS-CUSTOMER REUSE ≠ PRODUCT`; implementation discovery, mapping, configuration, approval, purchasing, and support variation remain.

## 18. What Chapter 18 demonstrates

Given modeled inputs, substantial engineering reuse does not compensate automatically for new-customer acquisition and approvals. This clarifies `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER`: same-account improvement weakens that hypothesis only locally; cross-customer commercial reset keeps it unresolved.

## 19. What Chapter 18 does not prove

Two fictional governments do not validate a real market, vendor, purchasing rule, cycle, price, or product classification. Sensitivities show mechanisms, not forecasts.

## 20. Why engagement-motion synthesis comes next

The unresolved question is how acquisition motions interact across opportunities and capacity. That synthesis belongs to a later chapter and is deliberately not implemented here.
