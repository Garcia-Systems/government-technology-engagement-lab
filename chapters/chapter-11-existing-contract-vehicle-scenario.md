# Chapter 11 — Existing Contract Vehicle Scenario

> **Fiction notice — MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION.** The Blue Heron Technology Services Path, its holder, eligibility, terms, and every rule below are wholly fictional. They are not claims about any law, contract vehicle, framework, schedule, cooperative, reseller contract, or public purchasing practice.

## 1. Procurement path as an experimental variable

Chapter 10 changed who owns access and the prime contract. This chapter holds the fictional customer, $104,002.80 recoverable annual value, $78,000 implementation price, $24,000 support, 522 engineering hours, and seller labor rates constant. It changes only the **purchasing path**. The question is whether the Formal RFP's `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER` result changes when redundant commercial setup is removed.

```text
SAME PROJECT + SAME CUSTOMER + EXISTING PURCHASING PATH
                         = DIFFERENT COMMERCIAL RESULT?
```

## 2. What an existing purchasing path means in this fictional lab

It means basic seller eligibility, general terms, insurance terms, standard payment terms, and an invoicing mechanism are assumed to exist. It can shorten specific procurement mechanics.

## 3. What it does not mean

It does not mean no procurement, automatic work, guaranteed funding, or automatic technical approval.

```text
PURCHASING PATH
      ≠
BUYER ACCESS
      ≠
TECHNICAL APPROVAL
```

## 4. Meet the fictional purchasing mechanism

The **Blue Heron Technology Services Path** is held by the fictional Blue Heron Administrative Services. The fictional seller is assumed eligible for workflow analysis, bounded configuration and implementation, discovery, and implementation support. Project-specific fixed pricing is documented in a statement of work. These are all `MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION`s.

## 5. What is already established

* Master commercial and some standard contract terms
* Insurance and standard payment terms
* General invoicing mechanism
* Basic vendor eligibility

## 6. What still requires approval

A departmental sponsor, scope and technical discovery, security/access and relevant accessibility validation, statement of work, project price, project and funding approval, implementation approval, and acceptance remain. Existing path **does not erase governance**.

## 7. Transforming the Formal RFP journey

```text
NEW PROCUREMENT PATH                 EXISTING PURCHASING PATH
OPPORTUNITY                          OPPORTUNITY
    ↓                                    ↓
SOLICITATION                         SCOPE
    ↓                                    ↓
PROPOSAL                             TECHNICAL / GOVERNANCE REVIEW
    ↓                                    ↓
EVALUATION                           STATEMENT OF WORK
    ↓                                    ↓
SELECTION                            PROJECT APPROVAL
    ↓                                    ↓
CONTRACT SETUP                       WORK AUTHORIZATION
    ↓
PROJECT AUTHORIZATION
```

Solicitation review, proposal assembly, submission, solicitation clarifications, evaluation wait, and competitive selection are removed. Technical documentation falls 24→12 hours; pricing 8→6; contract review 8→5; procurement coordination 8→3. Each change follows only from the fictional configuration, not a real rule.

## 8. Stage-by-stage effort savings

| Changed stage | Hours saved | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Solicitation review | 12 | No new solicitation assumed |
| Technical response | 12 | Project documentation remains; full response does not |
| Pricing | 2 | Project price only |
| Proposal assembly | 20 | SOW replaces full package |
| Submission | 4 | No competitive response submission |
| Clarifications / meetings | 12 | No solicitation clarification |
| Evaluation wait | 4 | No competitive evaluation |
| Intent / selection | 4 | Eligibility exists; project approval remains |
| Contract review | 3 | General terms exist; SOW review remains |
| Procurement coordination | 5 | Work-order coordination remains |
| **Total** | **78** | **192→114 seller hours** |

The stage changes reconcile exactly to total effort. Attribution shows the disappearing burden is procurement (41 hours), proposal (22), contracting (3), and technical-discovery response packaging (12). Buyer qualification, governance/security, and implementation planning do not disappear.

## 9. Elapsed-cycle savings

The sequential modeled cycle falls from 270 to 127 days: **143 modeled days removed**. Active effort and calendar delay remain separate results.

## 10. Customer economics

The path does not change price or value. First-year cost remains **$102,000**, first-year net recoverable value **$2,002.80**, and implementation-only payback **9.00 months**. This deliberately isolates acquisition mechanics.

## 11. Seller economics

Delivery remains 522 hours and **$57,420**. Acquisition falls from **$20,640 to $13,050**, saving **$7,590**. Acquisition-adjusted contribution rises from **-$60 to $7,530** (9.65%). Acquisition is 16.73% of revenue and 14.62 hours per $10,000 revenue. The result improves materially but remains below the lab's fictional $10,000 contribution minimum.

## 12. Procurement versus buyer access

Baseline buyer access remains `LIMITED`. Procurement is reduced, but the mechanism does not create a sponsor or a route into an opportunity. Security, accessibility, technical validation, funding, and acceptance remain independent constraints.

## 13. Scenario: buyer access still weak

Changing access to `NO` preserves the 78-hour procurement-path saving but yields target `FAIL` and `POOR TARGET CUSTOMER`. Procurement simplification is not access.

## 14. Scenario: nominal path with little benefit

Restoring a full proposal and substantial procurement/contract work produces 162 acquisition hours. A nominal mechanism removes less burden and remains unattractive/uncertain; the label alone has no power.

## 15. Scenario: strong existing path

A sensitivity reduces more commercial setup and assumes credible access, while retaining technical design, security, accessibility, SOW, authorization, and planning. This identifies the point at which the same project can become `PROMISING — VALIDATE IN DISCOVERY`; it does not claim that condition exists.

## 16. How much of the original burden was procurement?

Under this alternative, 78 of 192 hours (40.6%), $7,590 of $20,640 acquisition cost (36.8%), and 143 of 270 days (53.0%) are attributable to purchasing-path mechanics that the fictional path removes. Contribution improves by $7,590. What remains—114 hours, limited buyer access, governance, technical validation, and project authorization—is substantial.

## 17. Does the target verdict change?

The primary project gate remains `PASS`; target viability becomes `CONDITIONAL`; the Chapter 1 verdict is `INVESTIGATE`. Thus the original poor-target conclusion is weakened but not overturned by procurement alone. Strong-path sensitivity can pass; weak-access sensitivity still fails.

## 18. What Chapter 11 demonstrates

Purchasing-path friction was a material cause of the Formal RFP failure, especially through calendar wait and proposal/procurement work. The cookbook hypothesis becomes **more conditional**, not disproved.

## 19. What Chapter 11 does not prove

It proves nothing about real procurement availability, legality, eligibility, pricing, renewal, competition, or customer demand. It does not model throughput, repeatability, an incumbent alternative, or final motion economics.

## 20. Why security, accessibility, and governance come next

Those reviews remain explicit in this chapter. Their actual surface and consequences are unresolved work for a later chapter; Chapter 11 does **not** implement Chapter 12.
