# Chapter 7 — Configuration-First Government Engagement

## 1. Why custom-first is the wrong default
This fictional experiment holds the customer and **$104,002.80** recoverable burden constant, but asks what the incumbent can responsibly remove before custom ownership. Configuration is not free.

## 2. The fictional incumbent system
**CivicFlow Permitting Suite is entirely fictional.** Every capability is a `MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION`, never a claim about a real vendor.

## 3. What capability already exists?
The inventory models statuses/workflows, validation, views/queues, notifications, exports/API, scheduled reports/dashboards, roles, duplicate warnings, correction tracking, audit history, and simple rules. Cross-system reconciliation is explicitly `NOT_SUPPORTED`.

## 4. What is merely unconfigured?
The synthetic state has 11 inconsistent status strings, no required fields, saved queues, scheduled report, or notification rules, plus divided correction ownership (`MODELED ASSUMPTION`).

## 5. Standardization before software
Eleven source labels become six canonical statuses (`OBSERVED LAB RESULT`). Poor-standardization sensitivity therefore says `STANDARDIZE FIRST`; that is an operational instruction, not a new commercial verdict.

## 6. Configuration interventions
A compact sequence applies status standardization, required fields, saved queues, native reporting, notifications, and process standardization. Unsupported capability raises an error (`OBSERVED IMPLEMENTATION STRUCTURE`).

## 7. Native automation and reporting
Native reports and event notifications own only native fields/events. They do not acquire cross-source authority.

## 8. Process change
Single correction ownership and removal of a duplicate review step model burden removed without pretending software caused it.

## 9. Measuring residual burden
The new category allocation is a `MODELED ASSUMPTION`: duplicate entry $18,000; status reconciliation $17,002.80; reports $14,000; lookup $16,000; corrections $21,000; workflow rework $18,000—exactly **$104,002.80**. Sequential recovery applies fractions only to the remaining category balance, preventing double counting.

Thresholds are `MODELED ASSUMPTION`: below $5,000 `IMMATERIAL`; below $25,000 `NARROW`; below $50,000 `MATERIAL`; otherwise `BROAD`. These labels do not alone dictate a commercial verdict.

## 10. What configuration cannot solve
The fictional incumbent cannot perform cross-source reconciliation. Existing export capability can support investigation, but cannot be relabeled as native reconciliation.

## 11. The narrow custom edge
Configure → measure residual → if material, consider the Chapter 6 **read-only cross-source reconciliation view**. This is a candidate, not an implemented broad application.

## 12. Customer economics
The executable exposes addressed value, residual, percent addressed, $44,000 price, $6,000 support, first-year net value, and implementation-only payback.

## 13. Seller economics
Configuration, 24 engineering hours, 54 acquisition hours, delivery labor, acquisition labor, support, and contribution are explicit. Chapter 4 fully loaded rates are reused.

## 14. Configuration-first versus broad custom
Configuration-first has a smaller ownership and acquisition surface, but captures less theoretical value. Chapter 4, 5, and 6 remain unchanged comparison points.

## 15. Sensitivity: stronger incumbent
A fictional native reconciliation capability increases coverage (`MODELED ALTERNATIVE ASSUMPTION`).

## 16. Sensitivity: weaker incumbent
Removing native reports/notifications decreases coverage (`SENSITIVITY ASSUMPTION`).

## 17. Sensitivity: poor standardization
Capability remains available while fragmented process raises standardization effectiveness and effort.

## 18. Does BUY / CONFIGURE win?
Only if customer and seller gates pass and the measured residual is immaterial. The result is derived, not hard-coded.

## 19. Does custom still deserve to exist?
Only for a residual that remains material and independently passes feasibility, customer, delivery, support, and target gates.
